# Stress Testing in Quantitative Research

## Purpose

Stress testing evaluates how a portfolio, strategy, or financial system
may behave under unusual but plausible conditions.

Stress tests are not predictions.

They are controlled experiments designed to understand vulnerability.

---

## Why Stress Tests Matter

Normal market statistics often underestimate extreme events.

During crises:

- volatility increases
- correlations between assets often rise
- liquidity can decline
- diversification benefits may weaken
- losses can become concentrated

A portfolio that appears diversified in normal conditions may become
more exposed during a market shock.

---

## Common Stress Scenarios

Examples:

- 2008 Financial Crisis
- COVID-19 market crash
- Technology bubble collapse
- Interest rate shock
- Inflation shock
- Liquidity crisis

---

## Historical Stress Testing

A historical stress test replays a previous market event.

Example:

Question:
"What would my portfolio have done during 2008?"

Method:

- Apply historical asset returns
- Measure portfolio performance
- Calculate drawdown
- Evaluate recovery

Limitations:

Past crises do not perfectly represent future crises.

---

## Synthetic Stress Testing

A synthetic scenario creates a hypothetical shock.

Examples:

- Equity market falls 30%
- Volatility doubles
- Correlations increase
- Interest rates rise

Synthetic scenarios allow researchers to test conditions that have not
occurred historically.

---

## Important Metrics

Stress tests often evaluate:

- Maximum drawdown
- Total return
- Value at Risk
- Conditional Value at Risk
- Recovery time
- Correlation changes

---

## Research Questions

A quant researcher asks:

- What assumptions are driving losses?
- Which assets contribute most to risk?
- Does diversification survive?
- What happens under more extreme conditions?
- Which scenario creates the largest vulnerability?
